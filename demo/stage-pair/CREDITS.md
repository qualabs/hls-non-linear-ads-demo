# Credits and provenance

**Nothing that is on screen in this demo came out of an image or a video model.** Not the
advertising, not the race, not a texture underneath something else, and nothing inherited
from another demo or handed over by a third party.

That sentence is not held by looking at pixels — a picture cannot be inspected for where it
came from. It is held by the list below, which covers **every file** of `brand/`,
`content/` and `graphics/`, one row per origin. **A file with no row here blocks the demo.**
The last section is the audit itself: the tree against this document.

What is drawn here is of two kinds and no third one. Either it is **vector written by hand**
— an SVG, or a script that emits one — or it is **SPARKS**, a test title published by Netflix
under Creative Commons Attribution, filmed with a camera in 2017 and used under its licence.
Everything in `content/creatives/` and `content/race/` that is video is one of those SVGs
photographed frame by frame in a headless browser, and that bridge is the only way into those
two folders.

## 1. The programme — SPARKS (2017)

**SPARKS**, a Netflix Open Content test title, used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.
Neither the site nor the bucket states how it wants to be credited, so the attribution below
is written against the default the licence asks for, with its five pieces: the creator, the
copyright notice, the licence and its link, a link to the material, and a statement that the
material was modified.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Creator** | *Sparks*, produced by the Netflix encoding team, directed by Andy Schuler. Netflix, Inc. |
| **Copyright** | © Netflix, Inc. The licensor supplies no copyright notice with the file or beside it, so this notice names the party the work belongs to rather than reproducing one. |
| **Licence** | Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Public License — [creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode) |
| **Material** | `http://download.opencontent.netflix.com/TechblogAssets/Sparks/encodes/Sparks_4096x2160_5994fps_SDR.mp4`, listed at [opencontent.netflix.com](https://opencontent.netflix.com/) |
| **Modified** | **Yes.** What is on screen here is not the file that was downloaded. |

**What was changed**, so that "modified" is a statement and not a formality: the original is
4096×2160 at 59.94 fps, 229.888 s long, 0.4 GB. This demo carries its **first 198.0 s**, the
stretch before the closing titles. Where those titles start was measured and not assumed —
mean luminance of the frame, `signalstats` `YAVG` over the frame scaled to 64×36: the fade to
black starts at **193.0 s**, full black is reached at **198.0 s**, and at **199.0 s** the
value rises and stays put, which is the fixed `SPARKS` card and therefore the first frame of
the credits. So the cut is at 198.0 s: the fade to black is kept and the title card is not.

That stretch was scaled and centre-cropped to 1280×720, re-encoded to H.264 at 29.97 fps
(half of the source cadence, discarding whole frames), its HE-AAC audio re-encoded to AAC-LC
at 96 kbit/s, and the result packaged as HLS VOD in two-second MPEG-TS segments. The recipe,
with the reason for each flag and the measurement above, is in
`scripts/preparar-contenido.sh` and `scripts/empaquetar-contenido.sh`.

| path | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| `content/primary/` | the packaged programme: `index.m3u8`, its 99 segments, and the two signalled playlists written on every start |
| `content/.fuentes/` | the downloaded original, cached so that re-packaging does not fetch 0.4 GB again. **Not published**, and no page asks for it |

Nothing of the programme was generated by an image or video model, and nothing in it was
retouched.

## 2. The nine advertising creatives — `graphics/campaigns/`

**Ours, written by hand as SVG for this demo.** Vector primitives and text: **no rasterised
pixel of any kind**, no embedded bitmap, no external image, and **no output of a generative
model** anywhere in the chain that produced them. The reason each line of each file says what
it says is in the header of that file.

Three invented brands, one per sector, each authored in three shapes (ADR 0081):

| brand | sector | shapes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ZUMBRA** | a citrus soft drink | `graphics/campaigns/zumbra-16x9.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/zumbra-banner.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/zumbra-backplate.svg` |
| **KOVRIN** | trail footwear | `graphics/campaigns/kovrin-16x9.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/kovrin-banner.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/kovrin-backplate.svg` |
| **KETRAVA** | coastal travel | `graphics/campaigns/ketrava-16x9.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/ketrava-banner.svg` · `graphics/campaigns/ketrava-backplate.svg` |

**The three names are invented, and that was checked against a search rather than against
memory**: four candidates were dropped because a real brand of the same sector came back —
TARVO against the Finnish footwear maker TARVAS, STRAVON against Stravers Shoes, SOLVANE
against the destination Solvang, MIRALTA against Miral Travel. What each search returned is
written down in the phase's own record. None of the three palettes is the palette of a known
brand, and none of the three drawings imitates an existing logotype: the object in each piece
is the product — a can, a shoe in profile, a headland with a lighthouse — and no campaign
carries a brand symbol separate from its name.

**The typography is a system font stack**, metrically compatible and declared inline
(`Helvetica Neue`, Helvetica, Arial, Liberation Sans). There is no `@font-face` and no font
file of a third party inside these creatives.

## 4. The race and its six cameras — `content/race/`

**Ours, and drawn by a program.** `scripts/escribir-carrera.mjs` emits **one** animated SVG
scene — a circuit, six cars, three pairs of them racing each other in three places — and
seven framings of that same scene: the programme, which cuts between shots the way a director
cuts, and one camera per car that keeps its car in frame. Each framing is then captured and
packaged by the same bridge as the creatives. Here the authored thing is the generator, which
is in git; its output is not, for the same reason as the creatives' video.

| path | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| `content/race/svg/program.svg` | the scene, framed as the programme's own cut |
| `content/race/svg/caldrix.svg` · `content/race/svg/marvok.svg` · `content/race/svg/noctev.svg` · `content/race/svg/runtak.svg` · `content/race/svg/pentav.svg` · `content/race/svg/quentra.svg` | the same scene, one framing per car |
| `content/race/program/` | the programme, packaged to HLS: 120 s, 1280×720, silent |
| `content/race/caldrix/` · `content/race/marvok/` · `content/race/noctev/` · `content/race/runtak/` · `content/race/pentav/` · `content/race/quentra/` | the six cameras, packaged: 64 s each, from the second the multi view window opens |

**The six car names are invented** — CALDRIX, MARVOK, NOCTEV, RUNTAK, PENTAV, QUENTRA — and
they are the ones `demo/race-multiview/` already used and had checked. The colours were chosen
for separation of hue and luminance so that six boxes tell each other apart.

**There is no livery, no number, no sponsor and no driver of any real series anywhere in the
scene**, and this is stronger than a claim about intent: there is **not one digit and not one
letter drawn in the whole scene**. A car is a body, a colour and a shadow. No championship,
team, circuit or manufacturer is named or suggested, and nothing here was traced from a
photograph — there is no photograph in the chain.

## 6. Audio

**There is none.** Every packaged piece of `content/creatives/` and of `content/race/` is
encoded with `-an`, and no page of this demo promises audio. The programme of section 1 is the
only thing here that carries an audio track, and it is SPARKS's own, re-encoded and credited
above. Nothing was synthesised, generated or recorded for this demo.

## 7. hls.js

Vendored unmodified in `vendor/` of the repository, under its own licence
(`vendor/hls.js.LICENSE`). The demo loads it from there and does not patch it, which is the
whole point of the pair on `index.html`.

## 8. The audit — the tree against this document

The claim of the first paragraph is only worth what this check is worth, so it is a check and
not a reading:

```bash
./test/verificar-creditos.py
```

It enumerates `brand/`, `content/` and `graphics/` and asks of each file whether a row of the
tables above covers it. A file no row covers comes out named, and the run exits non-zero.

**A green run says nothing on its own**, so the script takes planted paths as arguments and
each one has to come out named:

```bash
./test/verificar-creditos.py graphics/campaigns/planted.svg
```

Run it before publishing, and run it after packaging anything new. Packaging is when a folder
appears, and it is also when nobody is thinking about a credit.
