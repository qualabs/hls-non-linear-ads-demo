What the window offers

The race does not stop. A catalogue opens beside it.

The commentators say the on-board cameras are available, and at that second the playlist opens a window announcing what else can be watched. Not a layout: a list of cameras with the name of the driver on each one, because a list somebody chooses from cannot be a list of identifiers. Below is the window of this run, read off the contract at the playhead — the cameras in it are the cameras that were packaged, under the names the race declares for them.

    A catalogue is allowed to be longer than the screen. The cap is of the grid and never of the offer: a race may announce more cameras than fit at once without anything being wrong, and what is capped is how many of them are up — never how many are on offer.

    Credits

    Every frame and every voice here was generated for this demo.

    The race, the cars, the on-board cameras and the two commentators do not exist: the picture and its sound come from Google’s Veo, the commentary from a text-to-speech model, and the cars were designed for this demo so that no real livery, number or sponsor is drawn anywhere. There is no third-party footage in this demo and therefore no licence to honour — which is worth saying rather than leaving the reader to assume, because the other demos in this repository do carry third-party films and do credit them. What each piece is and which model made it is in CREDITS.md, and the prompt that made each one is in the generator that sent it.
